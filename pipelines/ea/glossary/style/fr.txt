French style for electionsarchive.com

Write as a careful editor at a quality French newsroom (Le Monde's results pages, franceinfo, Le Temps) and a good French product team would: precise, plain, short, and consistent. Readers are in France, Switzerland, Belgium, and Canada; use standard French.

Register
- Address the reader with "vous" throughout, never "on" for the reader: "Choisissez un parti."
- Buttons, menu items, and links use the infinitive: "Ouvrir la carte", "Afficher plus", "Enregistrer". Never an imperative on a button.
- Labels, column headers, tabs, legends, and headings are noun phrases: "Participation", "Suffrages exprimés", "Répartition des sièges".
- Failures: "Impossible de charger la carte". Progress: "Chargement…".
- The site never speaks as "nous": no "nous", "notre", "nos". Rephrase impersonally ("L'archive contient ...", "... a été ajouté").
- No exclamation marks or marketing tone.

Sentences
- Less is more. Keep sentences short and in plain order. Never hang a remark on the end of a sentence after a comma (", ce qui ...", ", soit ...", ", faisant de ...", or a trailing figure or judgment); give it its own sentence or drop it. When the English is wordy, say the point more simply.
- Avoid heavy noun chains ("la mise en œuvre du calcul de la répartition ..."); use a verb. Split long English sentences.
- "race" is "scrutin", "élection", or the office; "course" only in election-night prose.
- Make articles agree with what a placeholder holds; when its gender is unknown, avoid the article or use a colon label.
- English "Today" on a label is "Aujourd'hui".

Placeholders
- {countText}, {share}, {percent}, and {margin} arrive already formatted ("1 234", "54,2 %"). Never add digits or a percent sign.

Typography
- A narrow no-break space (U+202F) before ; ! ? and inside « »; a no-break space (U+00A0) before :. Quotation marks « … ». No dashes as sentence punctuation where the English has none; an en dash stays in ranges.

Follow the glossary for every electoral term.
