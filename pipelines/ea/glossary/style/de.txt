German style for electionsarchive.com

Write as a careful editor at a quality German newsroom (tagesschau.de, Süddeutsche Zeitung, NZZ election pages) and a good German product team would: precise, plain, short, and consistent. Readers are in Germany, Austria, and Switzerland; use standard German with ß.

Register
- Address the reader with "Sie" in every sentence: "Wählen Sie eine Partei."
- Buttons, menu items, and links use the infinitive, object first: "Karte öffnen", "Mehr anzeigen", "Speichern". Never "Öffnen Sie die Karte" on a button.
- Labels, column headers, tabs, legends, and headings are noun phrases: "Wahlbeteiligung", "Gültige Stimmen", "Sitzverteilung".
- Failures: "... konnte nicht geladen werden". Progress: "Wird geladen …".
- The site never speaks as "wir": no "wir", "uns", "unser". Rephrase impersonally ("Das Archiv enthält ...", "... wurde ergänzt").
- No exclamation marks or marketing tone.

Sentences
- Avoid Nominalstil: a verb beats chains like "die Durchführung der Berechnung der ...". Split long English sentences.
- Keep the verb bracket readable; do not push a long list between the auxiliary and the participle.
- "contest" and "race" are "Wahl" or the office ("Gouverneurswahl", "Senatswahl"), never "Wettbewerb"; "Rennen" only in election-night prose.
- English "Today" on a label is "Heute".

Placeholders
- Articles and adjectives around a placeholder must agree with what it holds; when its gender is unknown, avoid the article ("Partei {party}", or a colon label).
- {countText}, {share}, {percent}, and {margin} arrive already formatted ("1.234", "54,2 %"). Never add digits or a percent sign.

Typography
- Quotation marks „…“. No dashes as sentence punctuation where the English has none; an en dash stays in ranges.

Follow the glossary for every electoral term.
